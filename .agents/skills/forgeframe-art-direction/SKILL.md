# ForgeFrame Art Direction

## Trigger
Use when redesigning a visible customer experience, building a hero, or choosing typography/layout/imagery. Do not use for unrelated backend tasks.

## Deliverables before implementation
- Identify customer, desired action, product truth and current site baseline.
- Audit 2–3 relevant references: Resend for typographic restraint, Linear for information hierarchy, Stripe for product proof and commerce, Mercury for credibility. Record *patterns*, not borrowed assets.
- Provide 3 materially different design concepts: editorial restraint; product-centered technical; intentional high-contrast narrative. Compare them against brand fit, differentiation, accessibility and maintainability.
- For each: art-direction statement, type scale, grid rhythm, focal element, real product visualization strategy, navigation hierarchy, mobile treatment, motion concept and anti-patterns.
- Present responsive prototypes or mockups for review before production refactoring.

## Hard rules
- One dominant focal idea per screen; do not assemble template cards.
- Visuals must communicate real product use. Never use a random fake dashboard or claim.
- The typography, spacing, imagery and motion should form one coherent system.
- Minimize gradients and glow unless explicitly justified by information hierarchy.
- Do not apply one composition to every product; preserve platform consistency but specialize flows.
- Keep PL/EN copy legible and allow long localized strings.
- Account for 360px viewport and keyboard navigation at concept stage.

## Handoff
Write a short art-direction brief, implementation tokens (mapped to actual CSS variables), 3 key screen specs, and open questions. Ask for concept approval prior to replacing live UI.
